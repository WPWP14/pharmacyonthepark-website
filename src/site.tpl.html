What is compounding?

What is low-dose naltrexone?

Naltrexone is an FDA-approved medication that, at its standard 50 mg dose, blocks opioid receptors and is used for alcohol and opioid dependence. Low-dose naltrexone (LDN) uses a much smaller amount, usually between 0.5 mg and 4.5 mg. Because no commercial tablet comes in these strengths, LDN is prepared by a compounding pharmacy.

LDN is prescribed off-label. Low doses of naltrexone are not FDA-approved for any condition, and compounded medications are not FDA-approved. Your prescriber decides whether LDN is right for you, the dose and how to monitor it.

What is LDN prescribed for?

Prescribers use LDN off-label for chronic pain and inflammatory conditions. Conditions studied include fibromyalgia, chronic pain, and some autoimmune and inflammatory conditions. Evidence is still developing, and results vary from person to person.

How does LDN work?

At low doses, naltrexone briefly blocks opioid receptors. Researchers think this may prompt the body to make more of its own endorphins, and that it may calm certain immune cells in the nervous system that drive pain and inflammation.

LDN strengths and forms

Our 1.5 mg, 3 mg and 4.5 mg LDN tablets are $60 for 90 tablets. We also make 1 mg and other strengths on request (call for pricing). We also compound LDN as capsules (including dye-free and slow-release), a flavored liquid for small or gradually increasing doses, and a topical cream.

Side effects

The most common are vivid dreams, trouble sleeping, headache and nausea, which often ease over the first few weeks. Some prescribers suggest taking it in the morning if sleep is affected.

Who should not take LDN?

Anyone taking opioid pain medicine (including tramadol) or who may need opioids for a procedure, since naltrexone blocks their effect. Tell your prescriber about liver disease and all medicines you take.

Product sourcing

It starts with the ingredient.

We buy active ingredients and bases from established compounding suppliers. Active pharmaceutical ingredients come from FDA-registered and inspected facilities, and each lot arrives with a certificate of analysis that documents its identity and potency.

Potency adjustments

We adjust every formula to the lot we're using.

No two lots of an ingredient are exactly alike. Instead of assuming 100% potency, we use each lot's certificate of analysis to calculate how much to weigh, so your dose is what your prescriber ordered.

Batch variability

When a new lot comes in, we update the calculation for that lot before we compound with it.

Salt and base conversions

When an ingredient comes as a salt or hydrate, we convert so the amount of active drug matches the prescription.
